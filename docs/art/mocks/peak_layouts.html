B. Sky Islets

A home island plus district islands

The plaza and Main Hall sit on your home island. Fields, Alchemy, Disciples and the Summit are their own small islands in the sky, and the bridges between them are roads you build.

C. Long Ridge

Terraces that climb away from the hub

Your land stretches outward in three terraces, each a step higher. The first terrace is small and open; Expand the Peak raises the next one behind it, so you always see the empty land you will grow into.

  • Neighbours and bridges stay where they are
  • Height gives views back over your sect
  • Growth reads as a climb, which fits cultivation
  • Farthest building is about 19 s from the gate
  • Only one direction to grow, so less sideways room

Same in all three: you build everything

Empty land at the start

No rim wall of trees and no roads on a new save. You see open lawn and sky, and the next land shows as dry ground with one "for sale" sign at its edge.

Roads as pads

The plaza ring, then one path into each district. Each one you lay raises your peak's Harmony bonus a little (capped), and the next costs more. Prices come from the economy check.

Groves as pads

A small grove per district (sakura by the fields, maples by the furnace, pines by the halls), also raising Harmony. Groves can be planted again for a fuller wood.

Two farmers per farm

Every Herb and Lotus Garden and Tea Terrace gets its own hire pad for two farmers, instead of three on the first garden only. Miners, alchemists and fighters also go to two.

What the research says

  • Open feel comes from land you can see but not use yet. Theme Park Tycoon 2 starts you on 4 of 64 plots and shows the rest as dry grass, with a "Land for sale" sign only on plots next to yours (TPT2 wiki). Restaurant Tycoon 2 and Retail Tycoon 2 also sell land in steps. So in all three options the next land shows as pale, dry ground with one sign at its edge.
  • Decoration pays through a rating, not as income. Retail Tycoon 2 averages decoration over the tiles customers stand on, and a better rating brings more customers (Retail Tycoon wiki, changelog). Restaurant Tycoon 2 gives a star per 7 nature decorations, with a cap (RT2 wiki). For us: roads and groves raise a capped Harmony bonus on your whole peak, so they matter but cannot replace buildings.
  • Repeatable pads get pricier each time. Idle games use cost = base x growth^owned (about 1.07), which keeps a count bonus from running away. Paths and groves can follow that.
  • Choice needs waits. A DevForum pricing thread sets each button at about 30 s of income early and 60 s late (DevForum). Choice only appears when buying A delays B (Game Developer). That part goes to the economy check, which is already looking at "no real choice".
  • Walking is cheap next to waiting. At 16 studs/s, even option C's 19 s walk to the farthest building is shorter than one 30 s wait. I found no published plot sizes in studs for the big tycoons, so the plans above are measured against our own map.
